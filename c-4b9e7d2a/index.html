What it is

Two sides. Senior gets paid first.

The same shape as private credit, with the collateral on chain: a senior claim on a pool of cash flows that swing week to week, and a junior that takes the swing and the first hit.

Senior · USDG

A thin vault of ours over Morpho's Steakhouse USDG vault on Robinhood Chain. USDG in, all of it to Morpho; our contract adds the weekly epochs and the first-loss hook, nothing else. Depositors get Morpho's rate plus first claim on Court income, paid weekly in USDG. Redeem at Morpho's liquidity. Senior never lends to the Court.

opens November · after audit

The Court · junior

Holds the Court's position in the votes on Fables (the chain's equities DEX) through our partner Sink, runs Bounty where those votes are bought, launches tokens, and collects the desk's fees. Pays Senior first, keeps the rest, takes the first loss. Everything it earns is on the ledger, by source, by UTC day.

Questions

Things people ask first.

What is the rate?

We do not quote one. Senior earns Morpho's rate, which Morpho publishes, plus whatever the Court pays it each week, which the ledger publishes. Add them yourself; we will not do it for you on a marketing page.

Can Senior lose money?

Senior holds nothing that can lose principal except Morpho itself. The Court's income can be zero in a week; Senior's claim is then simply unpaid that week. Senior never lends to the Court.

Who takes the first loss?

The Court. Its own capital sits behind Senior and absorbs Morpho bad debt first, up to what it holds.

Where do the votes come from?

From our partner Sink, the vote aggregator for Fables on Robinhood Chain. The Court is Sink's anchor holder; $THRONE lockers decide where the Court's weight points.

Why is the desk not the main thing?

Because a perp desk on a shared venue is a distribution channel, not a business. It brings traders and fees. The Court is where the fees, votes and launches turn into something a USDG depositor can hold.
